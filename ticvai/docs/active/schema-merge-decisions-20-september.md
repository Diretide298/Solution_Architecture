## 1 · The rental domain — 5 rows · **biggest item in the file**

**Both teams built rental in the same week, in different schemas.**

| | |
|---|---|
| ours | `rental.*` — **21 tables**, its own schema, authored 19 September |
| theirs | `catalogue.rental_rate`, `rental_requirement`, `rental_rule`, `rental_product_config`; `resources.rental_product_mapping` — plus four more already marked **keep ours** on the exact-name collisions |

Their own names give it away: every one carries a `rental_` prefix **because it is sitting in
somebody else's schema**. `maintenance.rental_damage_assessment` is our
`rental.damage_assessment`; `resources.rental_inspection` is our `rental.inspection`.

**Recommend: our schema.** Same principle they already accepted moving `branding` → `whitelabel`.
**The cost is theirs** — ten tables change schema on their side, nothing moves on ours (§ cost).

## 2 · The payments domain — 5 rows

Ours is `payments.*`, **16 tables**. Theirs is `orders.payment_method`, `payment_gateway`,
`payment_policy`, `payment_route`, `payment_terminal` — five tables carrying a `payment_` prefix
inside `orders`.

`orders.payment_method` is our `payments.method`. `orders.payment_terminal` is our
`payments.terminal`. **Recommend: our schema, same argument.**

**Note what we accepted alongside**, because it shows the disagreement is only placement: their
`payment_fee_rule`, `payment_eligibility_rule`, `currency_rule` and `deposit_activity` are all
taken as additive — into `payments`, not `orders`.

## 3 · The subscription domain — 2 rows, and we have two homes for it

`platform.tenant_subscription` is our `control.subscription`; `platform.subscription_tier` is our
`control.subscription_plan`. **But we also hold a whole `subscription` schema** —
`licensing_model`, `enforcement_policy`, `capacity_pack`, `vsi_assessment` and five more.

**Decide our own side first:** `control.subscription*` and `subscription.*` are two homes for one
domain, and that is our inconsistency rather than theirs.

## 4 · A `venue` schema — 5 rows · **the most expensive thing available**

They propose `venue.venue`, `venue.department`, `venue.outlet`, `venue.space`, `venue.zone`.

We hold **`platform.org_unit` as the venue — 91 declared foreign keys** — plus `platform.outlet`
(16), `catalogue.space`, `seating.zone`. We have no `department`.

| option | consequence |
|---|---|
| **keep ours** | `org_unit` and `outlet` are the two most connected tables in the package. **298 change points** |
| adopt `venue` | a cleaner domain boundary, and `venue.department` is a table we genuinely lack |
| split | take `venue.department` as additive, decline the other four |

**Recommend: the split.** The cost is all in `outlet` and `org_unit`; `department` is free.

**Their `platform.scope` is the same argument in a second place** — and there their name is
arguably better than ours, since `scope_path` is the column 58 of our config profiles address by.
We still decline it, on the same 91 foreign keys.

## 5 · A `pricing` schema — 3 rows

`pricing.dynamic_price_rule`, `_condition`, `_action`.

We hold `catalogue.price`, `catalogue.price_list`, `rental.pricing_profile`, `games.pricing` —
**and no dynamic pricing engine at all.** These three are a genuine gap, not a rename.

**The decision is where they land**, not whether. **Recommend: their schema** — a rules engine
that prices tickets, F&B, retail and rental cannot sit inside `catalogue` once F&B and Retail
stand alone.

## 6 · Payroll and HR — 7 rows · **flagged 18 September, still unanswered**

**Their `workforce` is a full HR system. Ours is rostering.**

| | |
|---|---|
| payroll (7, open) | `payroll_run`, `payroll_employee`, `payroll_line`, `payslip`, `salary`, `salary_component`, `pay_component` |
| HR master (7, **accepted conditionally**) | `employee`, `employment`, `job_title`, `leave_type`, `leave_balance`, `work_assignment`, `shift` |

Ours: `rota_assignment`, `shift_template`, `open_shift`, `shift_swap`, `attendance`,
`leave_request`, `staffing_rules`, `training_record`, `announcement`.

**The 18 September note says there is no requirement behind the payroll tables.** That is the
question: **are we building HR and payroll, or rostering against an external HR system?** Seven
accepted tables are conditional on the answer, and nothing else here is as large.

**Recommend: ask the client before answering them.** Scope question, not a schema one.

## 7 · Where a customer lives — 4 rows

Theirs puts customer, address, contact and family in `identity`. Ours splits three ways:

    identity.principal        who can authenticate      staff, partner, device, service
    marketing.guest_profile   who the guest is
    pii.subject + _contact    the personal data itself  separately governed, 47 inbound FKs

**Recommend: keep ours and say why.** The split exists so a subject-access export and a deletion
request are answerable by walking one subtree — ADR-0023. Collapsing personal data back into
`identity` undoes that.

## 8 · Loyalty — one rules table or four — 3 rows

They split `points_earning_rule`, `points_redemption_rule`, `loyalty_rule`. Our
`marketing.loyalty_programme` holds all of it; its own description is *"the rules of earning and
burning"*.

**Either shape works. We need one.** The ledger gap this exposed —
`marketing.loyalty_points`, against our `loyalty_position` balance with no history behind it — is
already **accepted** and does not wait on this.

## 9 · The membership hierarchy — 2 rows · **new on the second pass**

They model membership in three levels: `membership_program` → `membership_plan` →
`membership_benefit`, with `plan_benefit` joining the last two.

We have **one**: `catalogue.entitlement_template`, and it is large — validity kind, entries,
transferability, purchase eligibility, stored value. **The benefits are columns on it**:
`fast_track_tier`, `can_claim_shop_and_drop`, `included_value`.

| | |
|---|---|
| `membership_plan` | **already resolved** — it is our `entitlement_template`; we take their renewal rules |
| `membership_benefit` | benefits as rows instead of columns. **A new benefit stops being a schema change.** Real improvement, real migration |
| `membership_program` | the level above the plan, which we do not have at all |

**One question: does membership become its own hierarchy, or stay `entitlement_template` plus a
catalogue product?** The answer to `membership_benefit` decides `membership_program` with it.

## 10 · Cross-cell guest linking — 1 row

`sync.guest_link` against our `platform.guest_link` (5 FKs). Theirs is better placed — it **is** a
cross-cell concern and we already own a `sync` schema for exactly that.

**Recommend: move ours to `sync`.** Small, and it makes the schema mean what it says.

## 11 · `data_subject_request` is in two places — 1 row

`marketing.data_subject_request` duplicates our `platform.dsar_request`. **Flagged 18 September,
still true.** One of the two has to go, and the platform one is what the DSAR fan-out walks.

## The positions that are not on this list, because they are not per-table

| | answers |
|---|---|
| **Column prefixes.** They prefix every column with its table name; we do not | **645 of their 743 change rows**, plus 21 table renames already declined |
| **The bound on that rule.** Strip a repeated prefix only where what is left still names the thing — `approvals.approval_matrix` → `matrix` yes, `orders.order_line` → `orders.line` no | why we take `fnb.order` and `orders.discount` but keep `orders.order_line` |
| **En-GB throughout** — programme, organisation, authorisation, serialised | `marketing.loyalty_program` and anything like it |
| **Singular or plural.** We hold `sla_policy`, `admission_rules`, `region_settings`, `seating_rules`. **We have no rule and cannot argue theirs until we do** | `access.entry_rule`, `seating.seat_rule`, `platform.region_setting` |

## What moved since the first cut

**Three decisions closed themselves.**

- **Module enablement** — their `whitelabel.module_setting` is *"visible in the branded
  experience"* while `identity.enabled_module` is what the tenant **bought**. Bought, shown, and
  the catalogue of what can be bought are **three facts, not one idea in three homes.** Nothing to
  collapse.
- **The two match corrections** — `wallet.account` is our `wallet.wallet`, `whitelabel.menu_item`
  is our `whitelabel.navigation_item`. Recorded, no choice needed.
- **The four that needed their own check** — all four resolved.

**One appeared:** the membership hierarchy, §9.

**And one thing we conceded that was not on any list**: `fnb.fnb_order` repeats its schema. That
is the exact fault we ask them to fix in `approvals.approval_matrix`, and we cannot argue the
principle while holding it. 45 change points, taken.
