Where the screen inventory repeats itself

Four hundred and ninety-two screens are defined. Read by the contract operations each one declares rather than by the name it carries, they resolve to 373 distinct behaviours. The gap is not spread evenly across the platforms — it sits on eight workspaces that were drawn once per platform that touches them, and on one guest product that was inventoried twice.

01

What was measured

Every screen in the fifteen platform files was reduced to the set of contract operations it declares under apis[].operationId. Two screens with the same set are treated as the same screen: they read the same data, offer the same actions, and will need the same loading, empty, error and offline states.

Names, modules and platforms were ignored while clustering. Nothing in this report rests on two screens happening to be called the same thing — where a name collision is the finding, it is named as one.

Two thresholds are used throughout. Identical means the operation sets match exactly. Near-identical means they overlap by 80% or more on the Jaccard measure, counted only for screens declaring at least three operations so a one-operation screen cannot match another by accident.

Limits

What this method cannot see

Operations are a good proxy for what a screen does and a poor one for who is standing in front of it. A gate device held in one hand and a desk in the back office can call the same endpoint and still be two screens; the failure mode, not the payload, is what separates them.

Fourteen screens declare no operations at all — four of the eight accreditation screens among them — so they are invisible to the clustering entirely and are listed separately in the appendix.

Every judgement below about context rather than data is a reading of the one-line purpose field. That field is itself unreliable: 28 screens share one of just four sentences. Where a finding here contradicts what the team knows about the user, the team is right — the value is that the question now has specific screen ids attached to it.

What is not redundant

The check on the method

P06's Floor Service module looks like exactly the same kind of duplicate as the stock module beside it — ten screens over restaurant tables, sitting next to P08's F&B section. It is not. The two share two operations out of twenty-nine each. Table service is genuinely a different job from F&B configuration, and the module survives this audit unchanged.

The same test rescues the scanning cluster from its own numbers. Eight screens across P06, P07 and P08 share an identical seven-operation set, but a gate admission, an offline package status and a back-office scan log are not one screen. That signature is an operation list applied to a whole platform at once. The real finding there is narrower and sharper: EMP-010 ≡ SCN-007, EMP-015 ≡ SCN-008, EMP-017 ≡ SCN-014 — the staff app and the scanner restating each other, three pairs deep.

The test applied throughout this report: would one screen with a parameter serve both people, on both devices, in both failure modes? Where the answer is no, the pair is left alone and said to be intentional.

08

Six decisions, in this order

Each row is a question with an owner, not a refactor. Answering them is what removes the screens — nobody has to merge a YAML file to find out whether two screens are the same. Ordered by how much each one unblocks: the first two decide what a third of the remaining wireframes even look like.

01

Is the guest app a second product, or a wrapper?

Twenty screens are duplicated between P01 and P02, each pair declaring exactly the same operations. If the app is a shell over the same storefront, P02 keeps only the three screens that need the device and everything else becomes one definition with two breakpoints. If it is genuinely a second product, each of the twenty needs a sentence saying what is different on a phone.

20
02

Is there one order workspace, or one per platform?

Seven screens across the POS, the staff app, the back office and the partner portal declare the identical fourteen order operations. One screen taking an order id and a role replaces all seven; what differs between them is which actions the permission allows, which _schema.yaml already models on the component. Decide it before BO-051 and BO-070 inherit the same fourteen for a different object.

03

Is Venue Analytics a platform, or a report library?

Nine of its ten screens call a reporting operation and seven call both getDashboard and runReport; BO-029 Report Builder already builds them. If a saved report is a configuration rather than a screen, P16 becomes two screens — a library and a viewer — and BO-058/059/061, POS-008, PTR-018 and SUP-008 become entries in it.

04

Which app owns scanning — the staff app or the scanner?

EMP-010, EMP-015 and EMP-017 restate SCN-007, SCN-008 and SCN-014 operation for operation. Both platforms are offline-capable, and two apps queueing admissions offline against the same gate is a reconciliation problem, not a duplication problem. This one carries a correctness cost, not only a cost in screens: pick the owner, and have the other app deep-link into it.

05

Does the floor need its own stock app, or a food-safety round?

Seventy per cent of P06's stock vocabulary is already in P08, and two of its screens carry signatures identical to back-office ones. What P06 has that P08 does not is the HACCP set. That is two or three screens done standing up, not ten.

06

Are navigation landings screens?

BO-101 to BO-108 are the back-office section menu and BO-100 is its root. Under the four-states rule each owes a loading, empty-first-run, error and offline state. Either they are navigation and leave the inventory, or the rule does not apply to them — they cannot stay as screens nobody will ever specify. The same question covers the four Command Center landings.

Fifty-four fewer screens — the six rows above, added up. But the count is not the point. Fifty-four removed is fifty-four sets of loading, empty, error and offline states nobody has to write, and fifty-four fewer wireframes to draw and review.

Two schema fields would stop it recurring

sameAs: <screen id>

Declares that this screen is another screen at a different scope or on a different device, and names what differs. A screen with sameAs inherits states rather than re-declaring them; one without it that duplicates a signature fails the check — the way check-screens.py already fails an unknown operation id.

kind: screen | landing

A landing is navigation. It keeps an id and a route so links resolve, and it is exempt from the four-states rule — which is honest about what BO-101 to BO-108 are, instead of leaving nine permanent TODOs in the specified count.
