What to do about it

Six decisions, in this order

Each row is a question with an owner, not a refactor. Answering them is what removes the screens; nobody has to merge a YAML file to find out whether two screens are the same. They are ordered by how much they unblock — the first two decide what a third of the remaining wireframes even look like.

01
Is the guest app a second product or a wrapper?

Twenty screens are duplicated between P01 and P02, each pair declaring exactly the same operations. If the app is a shell over the same storefront, P02 keeps only the three screens that need the device — the dynamic QR, plan-my-day, and push — and everything else is one definition with two breakpoints. If it is genuinely a second product, each of the twenty needs a sentence saying what is different on a phone.

02
Is there one order workspace, or one per platform?

Seven screens across POS, the staff app, the back office and the partner portal declare the identical fourteen order operations. One screen taking an order id and a role replaces all seven; what differs between them is which actions the permission allows, which the schema already models on the component. Decide this before BO-051 Purchase Orders and BO-070 Work Orders inherit the same fourteen.

03
Is P16 Venue Analytics a platform or a report library?

Nine of its ten screens call getDashboard or runReport and seven call both, and BO-029 Report Builder already builds them. If a saved report is a configuration rather than a screen, P16 becomes two screens — a library and a viewer — and BO-058/059/061, POS-008, PTR-018 and SUP-008 Agent Performance & SLA View become entries in it rather than separate definitions.

04
Which app owns scanning — the staff app or the scanner?

EMP-010, EMP-015 and EMP-017 restate SCN-007, SCN-008 and SCN-014 operation for operation. Both are offline-capable, and two apps queueing admissions offline against the same gate is a reconciliation problem, not a duplication problem. This one has a correctness cost, not just a cost in screens: pick the owner, and the other app deep-links into it.

05
Does the floor need its own stock app, or a food-safety round?

Seventy per cent of P06's stock vocabulary is already in P08, and two of its screens carry signatures identical to back-office ones. What P06 has that P08 does not is the HACCP set — logTemperature, logColdChain, signCorrectiveAction. That is two or three screens done standing up, not ten.

06
Are navigation landings screens?

BO-101 to BO-108 are the back-office section menu, and BO-100 Venue Home is its root. Under the four-states rule each owes a loading, empty-first-run, error and offline state. Either they are nav and leave the inventory, or the rule does not apply to them — but they cannot stay as screens nobody will ever specify. The same question covers the four Command Center landings.

Fifty-four fewer screens — the six rows above, added up. But the count is not the point. Fifty-four removed is fifty-four sets of loading, empty, error and offline states nobody has to write, and fifty-four fewer wireframes to draw and review — on an inventory where 14 screens still declare no operations at all and the states are the part that takes the time.

Two schema fields would stop it recurring
sameAs: <screen id>

Declares that this screen is another screen at a different scope or on a different device, and names what differs. A screen with sameAs inherits states rather than re-declaring them, and one without it that duplicates a signature fails the check — the way check-screens.py already fails an unknown operation id.

kind: screen | landing

A landing is navigation. It keeps an id and a route so links resolve, and it is exempt from the four-states rule — which is honest about what BO-101 to BO-108 are, instead of leaving nine permanent TODOs in the specified count.

One thing this audit cannot tell you

Operations are a good proxy for what a screen does and a poor one for who is standing in front of it. Fourteen screens declare no operations at all — four of the eight accreditation screens among them — so they are invisible to this method entirely, and every judgement above about context rather than data is a reading of the purpose lines, not a measurement. The purpose lines are themselves unreliable: 28 screens share one of four sentences. Where a cluster here contradicts what the team knows about the user, the team is right; the value is that the question is now on a specific list of screen ids.
