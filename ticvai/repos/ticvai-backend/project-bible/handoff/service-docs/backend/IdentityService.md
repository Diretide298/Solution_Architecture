## Group: administration

### createRole

**`POST /roles`**: Create a role

**Start from a preset, then edit the checklist** (decided 2 October 2026, Chinmay; DEC-007; CHG-CSP-003). `presetCodes` names one or more presets (`listCapabilityTemplates`); their permissions are copied into the new role, unioned with any `permissions` sent, and the copy binds to nothing: changing a preset later never widens a role made from it. With neither, the role starts empty and the checklist is ticked by hand. An unknown preset code is `422 unknown-preset`.

**The role-time segregation check** (decided 14 October 2026, ADAM CR-079; CHG-IDR-003). The initial set is checked as `setRolePermissions` checks it: every `SegregationRule` is evaluated against the role's permissions, **whatever its `scopeSensitive`**, because every permission of one role is granted together at one scope, so a pair inside a role always overlaps. Only a rule whose `severity` is `block` refuses, with `409 segregation-breach` (`common.yaml`) naming the rule and both permissions; `requireApproval` and `warn` are accepted, and surface when a grant attaches the role (`createDelegatedAccess`, `createPrincipal`) and in `listSegregationViolations`. The status was `422` until r6 (approved breaking change BC-079). **The create is audited**: one `platform.audit_record` row in the same transaction, as `setRolePermissions` writes for a permission change.

**Tenant scope** (decided 14 October 2026, ADAM CR-076; CHG-IDR-001): roles are tenant-wide (`code` is unique per tenant, compared case-insensitively) and their rows are written only at the tenant root, so the operation is called at the tenant scope, as `setRolePermissions` always was (approved breaking change BC-076). A venue manager reads the roles (`listRoles`) and grants them; it does not create them.

**`ROLE_MANAGE` must hold at the tenant root** (decided 25 October 2026, Chinmay, ADAM CR-189 option A; CHG-R10ID-001; the rule on `Session.permissionsByScope`), read from the per-scope set: a caller holding `ROLE_MANAGE` only below the root is `403`, even when it holds another permission (for example `REPORT_VIEW`) at the root, which puts the root among its scope paths. Nothing is written.

### listPrincipals

**`GET /principals`**: List principals

**What a venue-scoped caller sees** (decided 14 October 2026, ADAM CR-076; CHG-IDR-001). `USER_MANAGE` is checked at the caller's scope, as before. Any staff grant may read `identity.principal` rows, so the database does not hide a venue's staff from a venue manager; the list is the principals whose home scope (`identity.principal.home_scope_id`, a `platform.scope` row) is at or beneath a node where the caller's `USER_MANAGE` holds, and `scopePath` narrows that further. A `scopePath` outside the caller's reach is `403`. A venue manager therefore lists the venue's people and those homed beneath it, never a sibling venue's.

**The reach is read from the per-scope set, never the flat one** (decided 25 October 2026, Chinmay, ADAM CR-189 option A; CHG-R10ID-001; the rule on `Session.permissionsByScope`): the reach is the scope paths at which `USER_MANAGE` holds after deny resolution, not the session's scope paths. A caller with `USER_MANAGE` at venue A and any other permission (for example `POS_SELL`) at venue B lists A's principals only, and a `scopePath` under B is `403`.

**Each item's `roles`** are read with it from the principal's live role grants (`identity.delegated_access` joined to `identity.role`; see `Principal.roles`).

**Ordered by `displayName` ascending, `id` as the tiebreak** (decided 14 October 2026, ADAM CR-077; CHG-IDR-002): a directory is read by name.

### listRoles

**`GET /roles`**: List roles

A role is a grouping for permission management — code, name, description and the permissions it carries (`Role.permissions`). Grants (`createDelegatedAccess`) attach a role or a single permission to a principal at a scope.

**No fixed default roles: the seeded sets are presets** (decided 2 October 2026, Chinmay, batch 1 ADM-554 and the pre-apply round; DEC-007; CHG-CSP-003). Chinmay: *"Default permission configs, so to speak. We still have checklists: if they select Viewer, for example, they can still give that viewer more permissions and rename it."* A new tenant starts with no roles. Creating one shows the per-module permission checklist (`listModuleCapabilities`) and the presets (`listCapabilityTemplates`: All, Viewer and Mid-level for each module, and the five starting configurations Cashier, Supervisor, Venue Manager, Finance and Tenant Admin that `docs/active/seed-data-proposal.md` section 2 drafted as roles, audit R229). **Picking a preset fills the checklist and binds nothing**: every tick stays editable (`setRolePermissions`), the role is renamed with `updateRole`, and `Role.presetCode` only records where it started. Who may promote or publish an AI model is whichever role holds that module's permission (ADR-0051, AI-D16).

`Role.isSystem` stays on the wire for clients built at r1 and is false on every role a tenant creates from 2 October.

**Roles are tenant-wide; a venue-scoped caller reads them all, read-only** (decided 14 October 2026, ADAM CR-076; CHG-IDR-001). A role is defined once for the tenant (`Role.code` is unique per tenant) and granted anywhere in the tree, so a venue manager holding `ROLE_MANAGE` at the venue lists every role of the tenant, with its own permissions, in order to grant it. Creating, renaming or changing the permissions of a role (`createRole`, `updateRole`, `setRolePermissions`) needs `ROLE_MANAGE` at the tenant scope; a screen shows those controls disabled to a caller whose `ROLE_MANAGE` is held only beneath the tenant.

**`principalCount` and `grantCount` are computed on read under row-level security** (ADAM CR-077; CHG-IDR-002): from the role's live grants in `identity.delegated_access`, counting only the grants and principals the caller's grants let it read. A venue manager's count is the venue's, not the tenant's.

**Ordered by `name` ascending, `id` as the tiebreak** (ADAM CR-077; CHG-IDR-002).

### openPlatformStaffGrant

### updatePrincipal

**`PATCH /principals/{principalId}`**: Update or deactivate a principal

Deactivation invalidates any live session immediately. **A change to `validTo`, to `primaryRoleId` or to the principal's permissions ends every live session of the principal at once** (decided 28 September, audit R126 (1)): the person signs in again and is resolved afresh. A change to `displayName` alone ends nothing.

**Every session and refresh token, at every venue, through one function** (accepted 21 October 2026, Chinmay, ADAM CR-180 option A; CHG-R10ID-006). The caller acts at a venue, but `identity.refresh_token` is its owner's alone (own-row; CHG-R11FXV-002) and `identity.session` venue-isolated, so ending them with the caller's own row security would revoke no token and leave sessions at other venues live while answering 200. So the operation ends them by calling `identity.end_principal_sessions(principal_id, ended_by, reason)`, a SECURITY DEFINER function in the same transaction as the update: it first checks the caller may update that principal (the principal's own write rule: the tenant root, or a grant over its home scope), then sets every `active` session of the principal `terminated` with `ended_at`, `ended_by_principal_id` and `end_reason`, and revokes every live refresh token (`revoked_at`, `revocation_reason`), whatever the venue. No grant or policy is widened. A refusal by the function fails the whole update. Any other operation that must end all of a principal's sessions calls the same function (`forceLogout` ends one session, at the caller's own venue, and does not need it).

**It never grants or removes a role** (decided 20 October 2026, Chinmay, R8 blocker cause IDENTITY-C15, option A; CHG-R8SPN-024). A role held at a scope is a grant: `createDelegatedAccess` gives one (role, scope, validity) and `deleteDelegatedAccess` takes one away. CMS-019's Change access calls those two for the roles it adds and removes, and this operation only for the display name, active flag and validity.

### deleteGuestAccount

**`DELETE /auth/guest/account`**: Self-service account deletion

Raises an erasure request rather than deleting immediately. PII is removed from the erasable store; ledger entries keep their opaque subject reference and stay intact.

**What blocks it, and where Identity reads it from** (ADAM CR-084, decided 14 October 2026, CHG-GDL-001). The request is refused with `409` while the guest still holds something a deletion would strand: an **open order** (`open-orders-remain`: an order whose status is `pending`, `held`, `partiallyPaid` or `paid`, the rule `orders#openOrdersForSubject` states) or a **live entitlement** (`live-entitlement-remains`: an entitlement whose status is `issued` or `partiallyConsumed` and whose `validTo` is still ahead, the rule `access#liveEntitlementsForSubject` states; a frozen or suspended pass is still `issued`). Identity is Foundation and reads nothing above it (ADR-0028), and Access is another deployable (ADR-0055 rule 4), so neither orders.sales_order nor access.entitlement is read: Identity answers from `identity.subject_holding`, its own projection of the order and entitlement events (`SubjectHolding`). **The erasure job checks the same two conditions again when it runs**, against the projection as it stands then; if a blocking holding appeared after the `202`, it erases nothing and closes the request unfulfilled, naming the problem type that held.

**The request is an Approvals request.** `requestId` is the `approvals.request` id, raised in the same transaction through Approvals' in-process `createApprovalRequest` (Approvals is in the Tenancy module, in `commerce` with Identity; ADR-0055 rule 3). A guest's own erasure needs no human approval step (ADR-0047 section 7); the tenant's approval matrix may add one, and then the erasure job waits for the decision. **It is raised for the guest** (ADAM CR-101, accepted 15 October 2026; CHG-R10ID-016): kind `configurationChange`, `subjectContract` `identity`, subject type `subjectErasure`, `subjectId` the guest's `pii.subject` id, with the guest recorded as the request's `requestedBySubjectId` and `requestedByPrincipalId` null, since a guest is not a principal.

**It enters the privacy queue** (CR-101, as for `exportSubjectData`). The call emits `identity.privacyRequestRaised` (request type `deletion`), which Marketing turns into a privacy request, so the request's `dueAt` applies; the statutory period is the client's to give. **The erasure job also deletes the guest's data exports**: every `identity.subject_data_export` row of the subject and its file in the tenant's storage, since an export on a signed link is a copy of the guest's data (ADR-0023).

**Every guest is erased locally; only a linked guest fans out.** The job tombstones the guest's `pii.subject` row (`is_erased`, `erased_at`, and `erasure_request_id` = `requestId`; ADR-0047), for every guest. Only where the guest is linked across cells (the session's `GuestSession.guestLinkId` is set; ADR-0010) does it also call cross-region `createDsarRequest` (`kind: erasure`, `serviceAuth`), which raises the cross-cell sync.dsar_request and fans out. A guest who never transacted across regions has no link (cross-region.yaml) and raises no DSAR record.

**The same job erases the guest's AI index, and the tombstone keeps it owed until it is done** (CHG-R8ERA-002, which replaces the later-release catch-up of CHG-R8RES-003; ADR-0047, ADR-0049). After it tombstones the guest, the job calls `ai.eraseSubject` for the subject (cell-internal, `serviceAuth`), which deletes the guest's index rows and search points and answers with the counts removed and left. The AI erasure is done when a call answers `rowsLeft` 0 and `pointsLeft` 0. Until then the tombstone (`is_erased`, `erased_at`, `erasure_request_id`) is the record that it is owed: a failed call (`502 provider-connection-failed`, no answer, or anything left) undoes nothing, and **the retry** is the job's own: it calls `eraseSubject` again for that subject with backoff, and its daily sweep calls it for every tombstoned subject, so a retry lost with a stopped job is made by the next sweep. `eraseSubject` answers zeros for a subject with nothing left, so a retry or a sweep only repeats what is safe. Neither this request nor its `202` waits for the AI erasure.

### linkGuestCheckout

**`POST /auth/guest/link-checkout`**: Attach a guest checkout to an account

Email or mobile. ## Group: identity

### changeOwnCredential

**`POST /auth/credential`**: Change the caller's own password or PIN

**`createPrincipal` defaults `mustChangeCredential` to true and nothing could change a credential**, so every new member of staff was issued an initial secret they could never replace: the first sign-in could not complete. `identity.principal_credential` was read by `login` and written by nothing after creation.
**The current credential is required even inside a session.** A till left signed in is a session somebody else can use, and changing its PIN without knowing the old one is how that session becomes theirs permanently.
Held to `setPasswordPolicy` (length, breach check); a PIN is held to the tenant's PIN settings only (`PasswordPolicy.pinLength` digits, `pinReusePrevention`; CHG-CRD-002), because it is valid only on a registered workstation (see `LoginRequest.method`). **Every other session of the principal is ended** on success, since a credential changed because it leaked is not fixed while the leaked one is still signed in. **What ending them writes** (accepted 26 October 2026, Chinmay, ADAM CR-242 option A): each other live `identity.session` row of the principal takes `status` `terminated`, `endedAt` now, `endedByPrincipalId` the principal itself and `endReason` `credential-changed`; the session that made the change stays signed in. The three columns are V0108's (CHG-R8MIG-015) and the CHECK that `endedAt` is null exactly while `active` is the next forward migration's: until the migration is applied to a database, the change that writes them carries the columns behind it.
**What it writes** (14 October 2026, CHG-CRD-001, ADAM CR-078): the principal's `identity.principal_credential` row for `method` takes the new PHC hash (ADR-0071), `setAt` now, `failedAttempts` 0 and `lockedUntil` null; the replaced hash goes to `identity.principal_credential_history`, which the reuse check reads; and a successful change **clears `Principal.mustChangeCredential`**, so the session that signed in with a temporary credential becomes usable. **A wrong `currentCredential` is 422 `current-credential-wrong`, not 401**: the session is valid and stays signed in; the wrong attempt counts towards the lockout like a wrong sign-in, and at the threshold the method locks (429 `credential-locked`). A principal provisioned by single sign-on (`Principal.ssoProviderId`) has no local password to change: `method: password` is 409 `credential-managed-by-sso` (a PIN issued to them by `resetPrincipalCredential` is changed here like anyone's).

### exportSubjectData

**`POST /guests/{subjectId}/data-export`**: Everything the platform holds about one guest

BL-149. **A subject access request has a statutory clock**, and answering it by hand across twenty-seven contracts is how a venue misses it.
**Assembled from the lineage, not from a hand-written list.** `api-data-lineage.json` already knows which tables hold subject data, so the export is derived rather than maintained — **a hand-maintained list of personal data is a list that drifts the first time somebody adds a column.**
Includes what is held and where it came from. **Excludes another guest's data even where the two share a booking**, which is the case that makes this harder than a table dump.

**The orders and entitlements it lists come from Identity's own projection** (ADAM CR-084, decided 14 October 2026, CHG-GDL-002): `identity.subject_holding` (`SubjectHolding`), kept from the order and entitlement events, gives each order and entitlement the guest holds with its status and validity. Identity is Foundation and reads nothing above it (ADR-0028), and Access is another deployable (ADR-0055 rule 4), so the export reads neither orders.sales_order nor access.entitlement.

**The request is an Approvals request, as for `deleteGuestAccount`.** `requestId` is the `approvals.request` id, raised through Approvals' in-process `createApprovalRequest` (ADR-0055 rule 3); the guest's own export needs no human approval step and the tenant's matrix may add one. Only where the guest is linked across cells (`GuestSession.guestLinkId`; ADR-0010) does the export job also call cross-region `createDsarRequest` (`kind: access`, `serviceAuth`), which raises the cross-cell sync.dsar_request; an unlinked guest raises no DSAR record.

**Which request, and who raised it** (ADAM CR-101, accepted 15 October 2026; CHG-R10ID-016). Kind `configurationChange`, `subjectContract` `identity`, subject type `subjectDataExport`, `subjectId` the guest's `pii.subject` id (`ApprovalKind`). A guest caller is recorded as the request's `requestedBySubjectId`, with `requestedByPrincipalId` null; a staff caller (`GUEST_VIEW_PII`) as `requestedByPrincipalId`.

**The export job and the file** (CR-101). The call writes an `identity.subject_data_export` row (`SubjectDataExport`, status `awaitingApproval`; `running` where no request is raised) beside the request. The job runs when the request is approved (Identity consumes `approval.granted` for subject type `subjectDataExport`), or at once where the tenant's matrix asks for no approval step; a rejected, withdrawn or expired request ends the row `rejected` with no file. **No matrix means it runs at once** (the lead, 25 October 2026, r10 round 3; CHG-R10ID-024): where Approvals answers `createApprovalRequest` with `no-approval-route` (no rule of the tenant's matrix matches the subject type), no request is raised, the job runs at once, and `requestId` is the export's own id (a UUIDv7 Identity mints), used the same way by the privacy case. The job writes the file in the asked format to the tenant's private object storage and sets the row `ready`. **The file is kept 7 days** from `readyAt` (r10 round 3): then the job deletes it and the row becomes `expired`; a guest who needs it again asks again. **The guest and staff read where it has got to, and download it, with `getSubjectDataExport`**, which issues a short-lived signed link to a `ready` file; no link is ever stored or sent by message.

**The statutory clock** (CR-101). The call emits `identity.privacyRequestRaised`, which Marketing turns into a privacy case in its queue (request type `access`), so the request sits in the privacy queue and its `dueAt` (the jurisdiction's configured response period) applies. **The jurisdiction is the country of the venue the request is raised at** (the request's venue, CR-198; the lead, 25 October 2026, r10 round 3), carried on the event, and **the case carries the request id** (`approvalRequestId`), so the queue and the request are one. **The statutory period itself is the client's to give** (`setPrivacyRequestTypes` `deadlines[].responseDays`); until it is configured the queue shows no deadline, and `estimatedCompletionAt` stays null.

**Which tables it reads** (CR-101). The table set comes from the derived subject-data marker, not from a list kept by hand: a table holds a guest's data when its declared storage has a `subject_id` column, and `handoff/subject-data-register.json` (derived each refresh by `tools/derive-subject-data-register.py`, r8 cause IDENTITY-C65, CHG-R8GEN-015) lists every such table under the module that owns it, so a new table joins the export by its own column. Identity reads its own tables directly; every owner above Identity writes its own part of the export, as Marketing does (below; CHG-R11FXC-005).

**Marketing adds its part; Identity assembles the parts** (the lead, 29 October 2026, r11 gate; CHG-R11FXC-005). Identity is Foundation and reads nothing above it (ADR-0028), so the export reads no Marketing table: on the `identity.privacyRequestRaised` this call emits, Marketing writes the guest's consent records, guest profile, form submissions and guest documents as its own part (`writeSubjectDataExportPart`) and says so by `marketing.subjectDataPartWritten`, which `recordSubjectDataExportPart` records as a `SubjectDataExportPart`. The job assembles the file from Identity's own tables, its projections and the recorded parts, and runs once every owner's part is recorded; it deletes the parts' objects with the export.

**An erased guest** (CR-101). A subject whose `pii.subject` row is tombstoned (`is_erased` true) is answered `410 subject-erased` and nothing is raised: there is nothing left to export, and the tombstone is the record that the erasure was done.

### forceLogout

**`POST /auth/sessions/{sessionId}/force-logout`**: Supervisor termination of an abandoned session

Requires SESSION_FORCE_LOGOUT. Exists because §3.1.3 rejects rather than displaces a second login — an operator who went home still logged in would otherwise be locked out until token expiry.
**Callable on the POS-000 door with no session, under a supervisor's PIN step-up** (Chinmay, 3 October 2026, Block A business rules: "POS-000: session management stays on the door"; CHG-RUL-015, which reverses CHG-DOOR-003 for this). ### grantDelegation

**`POST /guests/{subjectId}/delegations`**: Let one guest act for another

CF-132, CL-05. **A portfolio is delegated authority, not a household table.**
Covers a primary holder assigning entitlements, a group leader holding tickets, a corporate account enrolling members and a shared wallet with individual tracking — **four requirements the matrix raises separately and one mechanism.**
**It inherits the audit.** Who granted what to whom, when, and whether it has expired is already recorded for every grant, which a bespoke family structure would have needed built.
**This does not make the work smaller** — extending grants to guest-held, object-scoped authority is real work touching `orders`, `access` and `retail`. **It makes it one piece of work rather than four.**

**Who grants, and the minor rule — decided 28 September, audit R126 (3).** **The account holder grants**: the guest whose account the authority is over (`overSubjectId`), or, where that guest is a minor, the adult account holder the minor is linked to. A guest caller may not grant authority over anybody else's account; staff holding `GUEST_MANAGE` record a grant on the account holder's behalf. **A subject with no date of birth on file is treated as a minor.** The age below which a subject is a minor is an open value that client counsel sets; it is not assumed here.

**Built with a configurable age** (decided 20 October 2026, Chinmay, R8 blocker cause IDENTITY-C19, option A; CHG-R8SPN-024; R205 still open with counsel): a subject is a minor below `tenancy.RegionSettings.minorAgeThreshold` (18 by default, set per country), or with no date of birth. **When the 409 `minor-grants-adult-authority` happens**: the holder the grant is for (the path's `subjectId`) is a minor and `overSubjectId` is an adult, whoever calls, a guest or staff on the guest's behalf. **How the first adult link over a minor is made**: the adult enrols the child from GST-069 (R205), which writes a `familyMember` or `primaryHolder` grant held by the adult over the child, with the adult as grantor; this operation is how later grants are added.
**What a guest-made grant stores** (R8 blocker IDENTITY-C42, the lead's answer A; CHG-R8MIG-021): `grantedByKind` `subject` and `grantedBySubjectId` the granting guest (no principal); `scopePath` the tenant root (a guest account is tenant-wide); `effect` ALLOW; `permission` one of the guest-delegable list for its `delegationKind` (proposed, client to confirm): `primaryHolder` and `familyMember` ORDER_VIEW, ORDER_CREATE, WALLET_VIEW; `carer` and `attendee` ORDER_VIEW; `groupLeader` ORDER_VIEW, ORDER_CREATE; `corporateAdmin` ORDER_VIEW, ORDER_CREATE, WALLET_VIEW, WALLET_OPERATE; `corporateMember` ORDER_VIEW. Any other permission from a guest is refused 403. A revocation by a guest is recorded the same way. Staff recording a grant on the holder's behalf store `grantedByKind` `principal`; the SSO callback's group-to-role grants store `system`.

### login

**`POST /auth/login`**: Authenticate and open a session

One sign-in for every staff and partner door. **A browser door sends no `workstationId`** (the TICVAI Console ADM-001, Venue Management SUP-001, the partner portal PTR-001): a desk browser is not a registered device, and requiring one locked every web door out (CHG-DOOR-001, Chinmay, 2 October 2026: fix the Block A blockers now). **A device door sends it** (the till POS-000, the staff handheld EMP-001, the scanner SCN-001), and a device-bound `method` (`pin`, `card`, `rfid`) without it is refused 400 (problem type `workstation-required`), because the device is what bounds a PIN. Nothing else about the sign-in changes: the second factor is asked by permission (audit R135), five wrong codes lock step-up (R126), a second sign-in is refused 409 (R184), and several roles mean a role prompt (ADR-0003). Where the tenant has SSO, the same doors offer `startSsoAuthorization` / `completeSsoAuthorization`, which return the same `LoginResponse`.
**The credential is checked against `identity.principal_credential`** (`PrincipalCredential`, 14 October 2026, CHG-CRD-001, ADAM CR-078): the row for the principal and `method`, its PHC hash (ADR-0071), and its lockout. A wrong password or PIN adds to `failedAttempts`; at `PasswordPolicy.lockoutAfterAttempts` (password) or `pinLockoutAfterAttempts` (PIN) the method is locked until `lockedUntil`, and a locked method is refused 429 `credential-locked` without the credential being checked. A PIN must be `PasswordPolicy.pinLength` digits. **A principal provisioned by single sign-on** (`Principal.ssoProviderId`) has no local password: a `password` sign-in, or a `pin` sign-in before `resetPrincipalCredential` has issued a PIN, is refused 401 `sso-sign-in-required`, and the door offers `startSsoAuthorization`. A temporary credential (`Principal.mustChangeCredential`) signs in with `LoginResponse.mustChangeCredential` true.
**A device sign-in is recorded on the device** (ADAM CR-232, the lead's answer to r10 question 23, 25 October 2026; CHG-R10TEN-018). A successful sign-in that names a `workstationId` asks Tenancy's in-process API `tenancy#recordDeviceSignIn` in the same transaction as the session, so each device bound to that workstation gets a `tenancy.device_audit` row of kind `access`, action `signIn`, which `listDeviceAuditRecords` returns.

### refreshToken

**`POST /auth/refresh`**: Rotate the access token

Exchanges a refresh token for a new access token **and a new refresh token**; the one sent is retired (rotation). A staff refresh token slides: it lives `PasswordPolicy.refreshTokenHours` (12 hours by default, proposed, client to correct) from its last use, and never past the session's `expiresAt`. **A retired refresh token sent again is a reuse**: the server ends the session, revokes every refresh token issued from the same sign-in, and answers 401, so a stolen token works at most once (IDENTITY-C25, FOUNDATION-AI-C27; CHG-R8SPN-014; the rule is written once on `bearerAuth`).

**A guest's refresh token is stored as a staff one is** (accepted 15 October 2026, ADAM CR-100; CHG-R10ID-009): an `identity.refresh_token` row with `subject_id` set and `principal_id` null (exactly one of the two), so rotation, reuse detection and revocation (`guestLogout`, `endGuestDeviceSessions`) work the same way for both.

### selectRole

**`POST /auth/select-role`**: Choose a role for a multi-role session

Per 12 Aug 2026 §4 — a user with one role logs in directly; a user with several is prompted to choose. Resolves the effective permission set.

**A partner chooses a role the same way** (decided 2 October 2026, Chinmay, door follow-ups; CHG-CSP-001). A role is held through a grant, and the grant carries the scope: a steward at AquaCove Abu Dhabi and at AquaCove Dubai holds two grants of one role, and `availableRoles` lists one `RoleSummary` per grant, each naming its venue (`scope`). ### verifyGuestEmail

**`POST /auth/guest/verify-email`**: Send a verification link, or consume one

**Two ways to prove the address, one operation** (R8 blocker fix, IDENTITY-C38). The approved screens (WEB-013, GST-010, GST-073) ask for a **six-digit code**, so `mode` `code` takes it in `code`: checked against the guest's `identity.otp_challenge` row with purpose `emailVerification` and channel `email`, within the venue's attempt limit (`VenueSettings.identity.guestOtpMaxAttempts`); a wrong code is `422 email-code-invalid` with `attemptsRemaining`, as `verifyMfaChallenge` answers, and the last wrong one voids the code (`410 verification-token-expired`; ask for a new `send`). The session is the guest's own (`guestAuth`), so the code needs no address. The link path (`send` an emailed single-use link, `confirm` its `token`) stays for emailed links.
**Raised in review by Deep.** `registerGuest` creates an account and nothing verifies the address on it — and **a guest account with an unverified email can receive somebody else's tickets**, because a transfer is offered to an address.
Two modes on one operation: **`send` issues a single-use token; `confirm` consumes it.** One operation because they are one act with two halves, and splitting them invites a `confirm` that works without a `send`.
**An unverified account does not buy — decided 17 September 2026**, replacing *unverified accounts still buy*, which this description said while `registerGuest` and `GuestSession.isVerified` said the opposite. Tickets go to an address, and an unproven address is how somebody receives a stranger's tickets.
**On the guest web and the guest app, the site's `guestCheckout` feature decides the route** (requirement matrix 2.6.28 and 2.6.67 — guest checkout, configurable per site). It is **off by default**.
**The gate is the checkout page, not the cart** ([ADR-0045](../../docs/adr/0045-every-order-carries-a-proven-contact.md), 18 September 2026, matrix 2.6.1 §2.4: *"On the check out page, users can log in or manually fill in the following details"*). The cart is open to anyone; `checkoutCart` refuses until one of the two routes has completed.
- **Off:** the guest signs in, verified, at checkout. `WEB-011` and `GST-041` carry the fork.
- **On:** the guest may check out without an account, and proves the email or mobile the tickets go to with a one-time code at checkout (`requestGuestOtp`, purpose `verify`) — the *dual OTP validation* of the reference checkout the client walked through.
**Both routes produce a guest profile**, because the tickets have to go somewhere and the order has to belong to something. **A one-time checkout profile carries no credential** — recalling previous transactions needs access set up separately (matrix 2.6.29), which is what `linkGuestCheckout` does from the other direction.
**A returning verified contact attaches to the profile already held** (matrix 22.2.8) — the new order, its tickets and its history, automatically, with no duplicate record. **It does not open that profile.** The guest sees the order just placed and nothing earlier until they authenticate as its owner: a code proves someone controls the mailbox today, which is enough to route a ticket there and not enough to hand over a history. **Any match that is not a verified contact** — passport, national id, an unproved phone, a similar name — **is a suggestion for the staff review of matrix 7.3.7**, with its audit log and rollback. A merge takes the narrower of two consents (CF-160).
**The kiosk is exempt.** A walk-up pays at the kiosk and takes printed tickets, so there is no address to prove (matrix 2.1.10). An unverified account also cannot *receive a transfer* or *reset a password*, for the same reason.

## Group: mfa

### createMfaChallenge

**`POST /auth/mfa/challenge`**: Second factor at staff sign-in, and step-up for a sensitive action

Issues a short-lived token proving a factor was presented just now. Consumed by operations that require it — high-value refunds, ledger approval, permission grants, tenant termination.
**For a staff or partner principal it is also the second factor at sign-in** (decided 28 September, audit R135, R126 (5)(6)). When `login` answers `requiresMfa: true`, the client calls this with `action: signIn` on the pending session and then `verifyMfaChallenge`; until that succeeds the session is not usable. POS-000, EMP-001, SCN-001, ADM-001, PTR-001 and SUP-001 do this.
**For a guest only in a venue that enabled guest two-step verification** (decided 29 September, rev 3 GAP-B1, per venue, superseding the second part of audit R167). The venue is `venueId` in the body: for `signIn` the venue the guest signed in from, for a step-up the venue of the booking, cart or ticket being acted on (the calling client or service passes it). Where that venue's `VenueSettings.identity.guestTwoStep.enabled` is on, an enrolled guest uses `action: signIn` after a guest sign-in answered `requiresMfa`, and a step-up before any action listed in that venue's `guestTwoStep.stepUpActions`; the token is bound to that venue. Where it is off (the default) a guest caller is refused 403 `guest-two-step-disabled`. **A guest who signed in with an email code cannot use `emailOtp` as the second factor**: the same inbox twice is one factor.
A session that authenticated hours ago is not the same as a person present at the keyboard now, and for those actions the difference matters.

### verifyMfaChallenge

**`POST /auth/mfa/challenge/{challengeId}/verify`**: Complete a sign-in or step-up challenge

For a `signIn` challenge (decided 28 September, audit R135) a correct code completes the sign-in: the pending session becomes usable and is returned in `session`, or, where `login` also answered `requiresRoleSelection`, the client goes on to `selectRole`. For any other action it returns a short-lived, single-purpose `stepUpToken`.

**How an MFA step-up reaches the operation that demands it: the session** (Chinmay, 3 October 2026, defaults taken; CHG-RUL-020). A successful step-up is also recorded against the caller's own session, bound to the challenge's `action` and to that session, for the token's short life. Every operation that declares `x-ticvai-step-up: mfa` declares `x-ticvai-step-up-carrier: session` and reads the step-up from the session that calls it: the client sends no token field and no header, and a step-up made on another session or for another action does not count. It is single-use: the operation that consumes it clears it. Without a fresh step-up the operation is refused 403, and the client runs `createMfaChallenge` and this operation, then retries. A call with no session (`{}`) cannot carry an MFA step-up; a supervisor PIN step-up travels in the body or a header instead (`SupervisorStepUp`, CHG-RUL-014). The returned `stepUpToken` stays for clients built at r1.

**`GET /auth/sessions`**: List active sessions

Who is logged in, on which workstation, since when. There was previously no way to see this — only to terminate a session someone already knew about.
**Callable on the POS-000 door with no session, under a supervisor's PIN step-up** (Chinmay, 3 October 2026, Block A business rules: "POS-000: session management stays on the door"; CHG-RUL-015, which reverses CHG-DOOR-003 for this). Somebody else is signed in to the till, so nobody at it has a session: the till calls this with no token (`security` admits `{}`), names itself (`workstationId`), and carries the supervisor's own principal and PIN (the `X-Supervisor-Principal-Id` and `X-Supervisor-Pin` headers, since a GET has no body). The PIN is verified against the principal as `common.yaml` `SupervisorStepUp` says; that principal must hold `SESSION_FORCE_LOGOUT` at the till's venue, and the call reaches only the sessions at that one till (`workstationId` is required then). Anything else is `403 supervisor-step-up-refused` and nothing is read or written. **No session is opened** by the step-up: it authorises this one call. With a session (BO-053 Staff Directory) the caller's own `SESSION_FORCE_LOGOUT` applies as before and no step-up is asked.

**Ordered by `startedAt` descending, `sessionId` as the tiebreak** (decided 14 October 2026, ADAM CR-077; CHG-IDR-002): the session that began last is first, which on a till is the one somebody is asking about. **Only `active` sessions unless `status` asks for another** (CHG-R8MIG-015).
