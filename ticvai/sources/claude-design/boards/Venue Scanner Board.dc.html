P07 Venue Scanner — Access Control (11 of 11 screens)

staff · rugged handheld · 390 × 844 · must work with no network
SCN codes · board index · P06 staff app

One job, done forty times a minute: decide whether this person comes in. A venue-owned rugged Android handheld held in one hand while the other takes a ticket, on a gate that may have no signal all evening. It shares the employee-app visual language with P06 and almost nothing else — no tab bar, no home screen, no assistant. Riverside Arts is the stand-in tenant, as on the other boards.

Eleven screens, and the id gaps are deliberate. SCN-004, 005, 006, 010 and 012 were absorbed into SCN-003 on 18 August and each kept its copy as a named state. That is the right call and the reason this board has one centrepiece rather than six near-identical frames: routing to /access/admitted for something gone in 1.5 seconds is a page load per guest, and at forty guests a minute that is the whole problem.
Seven of the eleven screens carry an identical seven-operation list. validateAccess, validateGroupAccess, overrideAccess, lookupTicket, listScans, syncScans and getOfflinePackage appear on SCN-003, 007, 008, 009, 013, 014 and 015 the same way — the whole access contract on every screen that touches access. The consequence is visible in the layouts, not just the lists: the derived components were generated from those operations, so an offline journal and a bundle-download screen both got a camera scanTarget and a ticket searchField they have no use for. Residue in an operation list becomes residue in a wireframe. Frames below show what each screen actually does; the lists are annotated where they overreach.

Five outcomes, one screen, no navigation
outcomeAdmitted is readable at arm's length and gone in 1.5 seconds. outcomeDenied, outcomeBlacklisted, hardwareError and the offline strip are all states of the same view. That was the 18 August decision and it is the right one.
A denial is a reason and a next action, never a boolean
Already scanned 19:04, lane 1 is what the operator reads aloud, and it decides whether the next step is an override, a lookup or an apology. Do not admit rather than a red cross, because the instruction is what a person acts on.
Blacklisted never offers an override
It denies outright regardless of entitlement, and it is a separate state precisely so the screen cannot suggest one. A denied guest may be helped; a blacklisted one is a security matter.
The override is a sheet, and it captures a name
It is the operation most likely to be examined after an incident, so a reason is required and the principal is stamped on it. A modal because the decision is taken while looking at the denial — not a page you navigate to afterwards, by which time the guest has gone.
Hardware failure is not a bad scan
A dead camera needs a different response from a rejected ticket: one is try again, the other is explain something to a person. Two states, two colours, never one error.
Blacklist authoring removed 24 August
addBlacklistEntry and removeBlacklistEntry came off. A lane device that can add someone to a venue blacklist mid-shift is a lane device that can ban a guest for arguing.

Changing the mode does not change what this scanner enforces. A locked barrier still reads tickets; it just does not open.
This is a request, not a confirmed hardware state. If the turnstile is unreachable the mode will read requested until it answers — never assume the barrier did what the screen says.
Request free flow
Reported state and requested state are two different rows
Hardware control is out of contract and the vendor SDK is outstanding (CF-33), so the honest design is a request with an acknowledgement. The top block is what the turnstile says it is doing, with the time it last confirmed; the buttons below are asks.
Locked is not the evacuation mode
The instinct in an emergency is to reach for the most severe option, and locked barriers in an evacuation kill people. The label says which mode an evacuation wants, next to the one it does not.
Barrier mode and validation are separate
Free flow does not stop the scanner counting, and locked does not stop it reading. Stating that here prevents a supervisor using gate mode as a way to pause admissions.
setTurnstileMode is on two screens, both onLoad
It is the derived primary action here and also on SCN-002, and on both it is marked onLoad rather than onAction — a hardware write fired by opening a screen. One of the two screens should own it, and it should fire on a tap.
