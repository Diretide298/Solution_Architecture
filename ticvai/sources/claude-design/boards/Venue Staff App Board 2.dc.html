Board 1 got the person onto shift and through the scan and task loop. This board is the rest of what happens at the gate: the queue in front of them, a sale with no till, the assistant they ask instead of the radio, and the two screens that matter on the worst night of the year. Same dark shell, same Home · Tasks · Scan · AI · More tabs, same 44px floor. Riverside Arts throughout.

Twelve screens here, not seventeen. The split follows the work rather than the count: gate operations, the assistant, announcements and emergency belong together and stop at EMP-050. People, incidents and device settings — seventeen screens — are on Board 3. Board 1 holds the first sixteen. 16 + 12 + 17 = 45; the other 21 of P06’s 66 are hosted by the client packs.
Two contract problems are drawn rather than smoothed over. EMP-033 Capacity view carries createChannelCapacity, setChannelAllocations and updateChannelCapacity — capacity authoring on a steward's handheld, the same shape of residue that took createQueue off EMP-031 on 24 August. The frame reads capacity and does not author it. And EMP-037 Notifications, EMP-039 Announcements and EMP-047 Emergency mode hold an identical four-operation announcement set — three screens against one contract, and only the emergency one is clearly its own thing. Flagged, not resolved.

Steppers, not a grid of tiles
A gate sale is two adults and a child, priced from a list of four. Quantity steppers at 44px let one thumb build the basket without the tap-count of a till grid, and the running total sits above the pay button where the guest can see it.

First run is four real questions, not a description
An empty assistant that explains its capabilities in a paragraph gets closed. Four questions from this shift, tappable, teach the same thing and answer something on the way.
