Eight screens with nothing to reuse, and five that needed drawing

The last of the pattern gap. Each of these eight is the only screen declaring its pattern, which is why none had a frame to borrow. Reading them first changed what the work was: two are already drawn better elsewhere, one should not be drawn at all, and three declare a camera on a screen that has nothing to scan. Five frames here close the gap.

scanTarget is the most misapplied component in the package. The build plan asked why Interactive Seat Selection declares one. The answer is worse than a single mistake: three of these eight declare it — a seat picker, a QR ticket a guest holds up, and a footfall report — and none of the three points a camera at anything. On the two guest screens scanTarget is the only component not marked derived: true, so it was typed by hand while the rest were generated. On BO-060 it was derived from listScans and carries a written note explaining why a camera belongs there.

Phase 2 starts at BO-100
363 P08 screens, five boards by module, and the Venue Home frame above is the spine all five hang off. It is assembly rather than invention: the twenty frames now drawn cover the patterns those screens declare.
One pattern runs through every finding on all three boards. A generator applied a small vocabulary faithfully to 816 screens, and the failures are all the same shape: a component derived from an operation that happened to be attached, a state derived from a screen name, a permission sentence derived from a module that was never checked. The drawing found them because a frame has to decide what a component is for, and the YAML never had to. All of it is written up for the repository in notes/atlas-handoff.md.
