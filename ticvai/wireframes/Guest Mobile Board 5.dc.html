Slots are filtered by the curtain, not by the diary
The restaurant has tables at 18:45; they are not offered to somebody with a 19:30 ticket because two courses cannot be served in time. Hiding the slots that would fail is the whole feature — a guest who is rushed out of a restaurant blames the venue for both experiences.

Allergens are a requirement, not a nicety
The allergy chips are part of the platform and cannot be removed by a tenant; the free-text option exists because a fixed list never covers everything. What the guest states reaches the kitchen ticket and the table, not just the booking record.

One tray, everything on it
A programme bought here joins the drinks on the tray marked N2 rather than becoming a second queue at a second counter. The shop is four items because a guest sitting down eighteen minutes before curtain will not scroll a catalogue, and a long list depresses the take-up of the one thing most of them want.

Four systems, one receipt, no upsell
Ticketing, the restaurant till, the bar and the shop each produced a transaction; the guest gets one page. The morning-after screen is the most-opened in the app and carries no offer, because a receipt that tries to sell something stops being trusted as a receipt.
