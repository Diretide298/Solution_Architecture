Two findings whose fix is not where I said it was
F-07 cheaper than I said
The payment model already has the unknown state
states/payment.yaml carries pendingConfirmation as both an initial and a reachable state, entered by a timer when “the gateway stopped answering after the authorisation”, and leaving by inquirePaymentStatus to three distinct outcomes. One guard reads: “This is the state CF-63 exists for.”
authorised ──timer──▸ pendingConfirmation
  └─ inquirePaymentStatus ─▸ captured │ declined │ failed
No contract change needed. The screen is missing three states that already exist one layer down. This is a transcription onto POS-005, WEB-012 and KSK-008 — the cheapest fix on the board and the one that stops a double charge.
BUT · payment.yaml declares no offlineReachable while lineage marks createPayment offline: true and inquirePaymentStatus offline: false — you can take a card offline and cannot resolve it offline
F-10 my fix was wrong
Do not just flip the kiosk flag
I recommended setting offlineCapable: true on P05. The lineage says that would be a lie: of the 23 operations the kiosk calls, 15 are offline: false — including the entire buying path.
getAvailability · addCartLine · getCart · checkoutCart
acquireInventoryHold · inquirePaymentStatus · getTenantConfig
createGuestFnbOrder · reserveMerchandise · +6 more
The honest fix is narrower. The kiosk does not need offline capability; it needs a declared degraded mode — sell nothing, resolve what is in flight, print the reference, call staff. That is one state on 17 screens, not an offline bundle. Flipping the flag would oblige 15 operations to work offline that were never built to.
What the standard audit has to check, in this order
1 · downward
Every destructive button resolves to an operation; every operation to a contract, a service, a permission and a write set. Two of 39 name no operation — those are the first to fail.
2 · state
Every write that changes a status has a declared transition. 6 of 15 do not, so the dialog cannot say what the record becomes — and neither can the handler.
3 · cascade
Every transition that moves a second entity is named. cancelPerformance moves an Entitlement owned by another context; cancelFnbOrder kills a Kitchen ticket. Both belong in the body.
4 · fan-out
Every cascade has an event, or a stated reason it does not. 10 of 11 have neither. One of the two that does reaches 5 consumers in 5 contexts.
Run it in that order and the dialog copy falls out of it. “Cancel 312 tickets worth AED 24,960” is not a writing task — it is the entitlement cascade, the refund consumer and the void-at-gate consumer, read off the chain and put in a sentence. The audit is the prerequisite for the fix, not a follow-up to it — which is why the 31 August pass could only produce one string.
