Board 1 is the sale. This board is everything around it: collecting a booking made elsewhere, the two hardware failures a kiosk actually has, calling a human, and the two secondary paths a venue switches on once the ticketing works. Frame ids are the client's reference codes, so every row in the index links straight here.

Order of information is the design
Paid, then where the tickets are, then the reference, then how to get them digitally. A guest who reads only the first line still knows the thing that matters.

Card-as-identity, with nothing charged
A zero-value read returns the token the booking was made with. It is the fastest path for a guest whose email is buried in a phone with a dead battery, and it is stated on the tile that no money moves.
The scanner is lit and aimed
The dashed target on screen sits at the same height as the physical window, and the window illuminates when this screen opens. Guests hold codes to the screen otherwise.
No booking found is not a dead end
Two failed attempts offers staff help directly rather than a third try. Names and postcodes are never used as lookups on an unattended machine.

The call carries context
Staff receive the kiosk id, the screen the guest was on, the basket and the payment state. They arrive knowing the problem instead of asking the guest to explain it standing up.
Waiting is not a modal
The guest can return to what they were doing with the call still open. Trapping somebody on a "please wait" screen for two minutes is how kiosk sessions get abandoned.
The estimate is real or absent
It comes from median response time on this kiosk in this hour. With no data the line reads "as soon as we can" rather than an invented number.

Partial failure keeps partial service
An empty paper roll stops selling but not collecting on a phone; a dead reader stops selling but not collection printing. The screen names which half still works rather than closing everything.
Directions are physical
"By Door 3, about 30 metres" is configured per kiosk during install. A guest cannot use a floor plan they are standing in front of.
The next kiosk is checked, not assumed
It is only offered when that machine reports healthy. Sending a queue to a second broken kiosk is worse than sending them to the desk.

The button states the collection time
"Pay and collect at 20:35" rather than "Pay". The time comes from the session's interval and the bar's own capacity for that interval, so it can be relied on.
Sold-out items stay listed
Greyed with a reason, in place. A guest looking for the ice cream advertised outside needs to see that it is unavailable, not wonder if they missed it.
Allergens are handled by the bar
A kiosk cannot take responsibility for an allergy conversation. It prints the information and points at a person; it does not ask a guest to self-certify on glass.

Six items, not a catalogue
The tenant chooses up to six lines for the kiosk from the full retail catalogue. The kiosk is a till at a door, not a shop — anything needing a size or a colour choice belongs in the web storefront.
Collection, not carrying

Stock is live or the line is hidden
The counts come from the same inventory the retail till reads. A limited run shows its remaining number; ordinary stock shows nothing.

KSK-017Assistantanswers, then hands over
Four questions guests actually ask a machine, answered from venue data. It never takes a payment and never guesses — the fallback is a person, not a longer answer.

Riverside Arts · tonight

Is there step-free access?
What time does it finish?
Twelfth Night ends at about 22:10, including a 20-minute interval at 20:35.
Can I bring a bag?
Anything about your booking or your money goes to a member of staff.
Talk to a person
A fixed set, not a chat box
Four to six configured questions with answers drawn from venue and session data. An open text field on a foyer kiosk invites abuse and produces answers nobody can stand behind.
Bookings and money are out of scope
Stated on the screen. Anything touching a specific order, refund or charge routes to KSK-013 without attempting an answer.
Answers cite the session
"About 22:10, including a 20-minute interval at 20:35" comes from the schedule record, so it changes with a running-late performance rather than repeating a printed time.
