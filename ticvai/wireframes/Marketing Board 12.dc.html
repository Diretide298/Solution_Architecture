Waivers exist for the day something goes wrong, which means they are only worth collecting if you can later prove exactly what a person was shown and when. Two rules follow from that. Every signature keeps the rendered document, not a reference to a template that has since changed; and a waiver may never be the reason someone is turned away at the door.

✓The rendered document as it was shown
✓Version number and content hash
✓Which acknowledgements were ticked, in order
✓Timestamp, device and IP
×A pointer to the current template

How the school does it
One link goes to the trip organiser, who forwards it to thirty parents. Each parent opens it, sees only their own child, and signs. The organiser sees a count, never another family's details.
Before this, a teacher signed thirty times on a coach. It was faster and it was worthless as evidence, because no parent had seen the document.
Who may sign for whom
✓A parent or guardian, for a child under 18
✓A named carer, where the guest's record says so
✓A teacher, with a school letter on file
×An adult, for another adult
×A booker, for guests they cannot name
×Staff, on a guest's behalf, for any reason
The last one is the rule most often asked about at a busy gate. There is no override, because a steward-signed waiver is a forged document.
Passing the link on
Link is per person, not per bookingyes
Forwardable by the bookeryes
Shows other guests' datanever
Expiresat the end of the event
Forty-four per cent of unsigned waivers belong to someone who was booked for by a friend. A forwardable per-person link is the fix for most of tonight's 318.
The person who bears the risk signs
Every convenient shortcut here — a booker signing for the party, a teacher for the coach, a steward for the queue — produces a document that will not hold. Making the link easy to forward is the only honest shortcut.

What changed in March
1The waiver link moved into the confirmation email, above the tickets rather than in a footer. That single change accounted for most of the gain.
2The day-before message stopped being a waiver reminder and became a "getting here" message that happens to contain the waiver.
3Links became per person and forwardable, so a booker could pass them to friends.
4The one-hour push was removed. It annoyed people on trains and converted almost nobody.
Completion went from 44% to 85% with one fewer message than before. The gain came from placement and framing, not from asking more often.
