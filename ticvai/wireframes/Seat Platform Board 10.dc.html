A season ticket is a promise about a specific seat across a year of performances, which makes it the hardest thing in this module to keep. Maps change, productions kill rows, prices rise, and a subscriber notices all three. This board treats a subscription as inventory held for a named person: renewed in place, moved with consent, and priced so the discount is visible to both sides rather than buried in a spreadsheet somebody maintains by hand.

Offers are made ten at a time so that a declined offer can be re-matched before the next batch goes out. Nobody is offered a seat that somebody ahead of them might still take.
How an equivalent is chosen
Same relative position first
An aisle seat is replaced by an aisle seat; a centre seat by a centre seat. Subscribers describe their seat by its position, not its number.
Then distance
One row back is a small change; six rows back is a different seat and needs a conversation.
Never a worse band
If only a cheaper band is free, the price drops with it. Nobody pays band A for a band B seat.
Access facts preserved
Step count, level entry and companion adjacency must match or improve, without exception.
Eleven of fourteen offers have been accepted first time because the offer was recognisably the same seat somewhere else.
What the letter says
A camera platform has been built where row D used to be, so your seats D6 and D7 no longer exist. We have held E6 and E7 for you — one row further back, same two seats in from the aisle, same price. They are yours unless you tell us otherwise by 20 September, and we can look at other seats with you if you would prefer.

A free exchange is a resale
When a subscriber swaps out of a Thursday into a Friday, the Thursday seat returns to sale — usually into a night that needs it less, but sometimes into a sold-out one. Last season 1 840 exchanges released 1 840 seats, of which 1 240 resold. The exchange cost the house nothing and earned £58 000.

The only two rules
1Twenty-four hours' notice, so the released seat has time to sell.
2Same production, same band. Moving up a band costs the difference.
No limit on the number of exchanges, no fee, no form. Every additional rule would produce more phone calls than it prevents.

Eighteen seats match twelve of the eighty-four. Nobody further down the list is offered a seat somebody above them wanted, and everybody is told their position whether or not they get an offer.
The published rules
Order is time on the list
Not spend, not donation level, not who knows the director. Seniority as a subscriber breaks ties.
One offer per released seat
Seven days to accept. Declining keeps your place; two declines moves you to the back.
Access requests are matched first
Wheelchair spaces are scarce, so an access request takes the first suitable space regardless of list position.
Position is visible
Every member on the list can see their number in their account, all year.
A waiting list whose rules are not published is a list people assume is fixed. Publishing them is what makes waiting four seasons tolerable.
No wheelchair space to offer
The third person on the list has waited two seasons for a stalls space. None of the fourteen has come free, because the access season renews at 95%. The honest answer is that they will not get one unless the house builds more.
