A seat map is the single source of truth for what can be sold, who can sit where, and what a guest sees before they pay. Two rules shape this board. Every seat carries the physical facts about itself — sightline, step count, width — because those are what a guest is really buying; and a map cannot be published until it matches the building, checked against the fire plan rather than against the last version of the map.

Why distances are stored per seat
A guest asking "how far to the nearest toilet" is usually asking whether they can manage the evening. Sixty-four metres and fifty-four steps is a different answer from sixty-four metres on the level, and both are on the seat record.
Removed, not restricted
A camera platform where seats used to be is not a restricted view. Those twelve seats must leave the map, because a restricted seat is still sellable and a seat that does not exist is not.
