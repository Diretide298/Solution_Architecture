Between doors and curtain, two thousand people enter a building through four doors with no mobile signal. Scanning has to work offline, decide in under a second, and never make an usher choose between holding up a queue and admitting a ticket they are unsure about. Everything on this board is designed for a person standing up, in dim light, with a queue in front of them — and for the duty manager who needs to know how full the room actually is.

Six of the eighteen can be seated now without anybody noticing: level-entry spaces, rear rows and aisle ends. Holding them in the foyer for fifty minutes because of a blanket rule is a worse outcome for everyone.
The rule, per production
Twelfth NightInterval onlyexcept level-entry, rear and aisle-end seats
Set byCompany managerrecorded at performance setup
The exception list is agreed once with the production rather than argued at each performance. An usher never has to decide alone whether a guest is disruptive enough to hold.
What the foyer screen says
The performance has started. If your seat is in the rear stalls or on an aisle we can seat you now — please speak to an usher. Everyone else will be seated at the interval, at about 20:35. There is a screen and sound in the bar.
shown on the foyer screens automatically at curtain
Access needs come first
A guest who needs a level entry or a hearing loop seat is seated immediately if it can be done without crossing a row. Making somebody with access needs wait fifty minutes because they were late is the wrong answer.
18 is normal
About 0.8% of a house arrives after curtain, and it rises to 3% when there is a rail disruption. The interval seating is planned for, not improvised.
