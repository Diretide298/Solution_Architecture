Two attempts was three until June. Dropping it to two raised satisfaction on handed-over chats from 3.1 to 4.2 out of 5 and cost nothing — the third attempt almost never worked.
How it identifies itself
It opens with "I'm an assistant, not a person" and never uses a human name or an avatar. A guest who thinks they are talking to staff and then discovers otherwise complains about the deception rather than the answer.
It never answers accessibility questions
A wrong answer about a step-free route means a guest cannot get to their seat. 180 of these a week go straight to a person, and that is the correct cost.
Acting is narrower than answering
The bot can resend tickets and move a booking to an identically priced session. It cannot refund, cannot change a price, and cannot touch a group booking — anything with money attached needs a person.

How a conversation is routed
1Accessibility mentioned · straight to the trained queue
2A complaint · to the complaints queue whatever the channel
3A group account · to the sales owner for that account
4Previously handled by an agent · back to the same agent
5Otherwise · to the channel queue
Rule 5 is the problem. Everything that does not match rules 1 to 4 falls into a channel queue, and the email channel queue has nobody rostered to it — 88 conversations deep with a six-day tail.
Nobody is on email
Four agents are on chat, which clears in four minutes. Moving one to email would clear 88 conversations in roughly three hours and take chat to six minutes.
Move one agent
Why chat is fully staffed
A guest waiting in chat is visibly waiting and complains within minutes. An email backlog is invisible to everyone except the guest, so staffing drifts toward the queue that shouts.
Accessibility routes first
It is rule one, above complaints, because a guest asking whether they can get to their seat has a visit to plan and only a trained agent should answer. Six waiting, longest eleven minutes.
