Three facts per row, chosen deliberately
Cheapest price, rough availability and access provision are what decide a date. Putting them in the list rather than behind a tap removes most of the back-and-forth: a guest who needs a captioned performance sees the only two on offer without opening nine screens.

Sold out stays visible
A sold-out performance is dimmed rather than removed, and offers the waiting list. Deleting it from the list makes a guest think they misremembered the run, and loses the demand signal that decides whether a date is added.

Best, nearest, cheapest — and why each one
Three offers along the axes guests actually argue about, each with the distance, the steps and the view stated. Two thirds take one of the three and never open the map, which on a 390-point screen is the difference between a thirty-second purchase and a two-minute pinch-and-drag.

Restricted view is stated, not discounted quietly
J18 and J19 are cheap because stage left is partly hidden, and the offer says so in the same size type as the price. A guest who discovers the pillar in the auditorium asks for a refund; one who was told chose it.

Navigate by block, not by zoom
The desktop seat map is one canvas the guest zooms into. On a phone that produces mis-taps and lost orientation, so the map is broken into blocks and the guest steps into one — eight seats across, thirty points each, five rows visible. Every seat is a legitimate touch target and the pinch gesture is never needed.

The touch area exceeds the drawn seat, so the gap between seats belongs to the nearer one rather than to nothing.
The summary line carries the facts
A seat on a phone map cannot show its own distance, steps or view, so the strip above the button states them for the current selection. It is the same sentence the offer cards use, which is what lets a guest compare a map pick against the best-available offer.

The £7.50 fee was in the price on the date list and in the offer card, so the payment screen introduces nothing. Fee-at-checkout is the single largest cause of abandonment on the old web flow, and the platform forbids it: a tenant may set any fee it likes and must name it before the seat is held.
