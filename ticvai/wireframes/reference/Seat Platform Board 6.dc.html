A performance is a map, a price grid, a set of holds and a moment when the doors open online. Most on-sale failures are not technical: a title goes live with last season's map, or with prices approved for a different run, or with a queue nobody tested against a genuinely popular show. This board puts everything an on-sale depends on in one checklist, then watches the first ten minutes closely enough to intervene.

What we learned
written by the on-sale team, 4 Sep
1
Band C was priced below demand
The cheapest band cleared in 92 seconds on both Saturdays while band A sat 60% unsold. £30 was the wrong number for 600 seats with a full view.
pricing
2
The midweek diversion worked
Offering uncontested dates inside the queue sold 3 120 seats and shortened the wait for everyone else by six minutes. Make it standard.
keep
3
One payment provider degraded at peak
418 guests saw an error. Failover took nine minutes because nobody was sure who was allowed to press it. Name that person before the next on-sale.
process
4
Map v12 was published 40 minutes before opening
It worked, and it should never happen again. The checklist caught it; the timing was luck.
process
5
42 access bookings, none needed a phone call
First on-sale where every wheelchair space was booked online with a companion seat attached. The queue bypass was used 42 times and complained about zero times.
keep
6
A third Saturday matinee would have sold
Both Saturday evenings cleared in minutes and the matinees reached 52% on day one. The room is free on 26 Sep.
programming
Six notes, written the same week, each with an owner. The value is entirely in writing them before the reasons are forgotten.
