Every other board in this module tries to prevent surprises. This one handles them: a row lost to a rig change, a lift out of service, a venue swap, a whole house to move at four hours' notice. The measure of a good reseat is that the guest hears it from the venue rather than from an usher, gets an equal or better seat, and is not asked to do any work. Everything here is built to be finished in one sitting by one person.

How each item is judged
Can it be fixed?
A repair before the performance beats every reseat. Ask this first and put a number on it.
Can everyone be moved?
Equal or better, party intact, access facts preserved. If not, some guests need a refund, not a seat.
How much notice is left?
Under 48 hours changes everything: the notice goes by text, and compensation is assumed rather than argued.
Three questions, asked in that order, on every disruption. Most of the damage in past incidents came from answering the second before the first.

Open the 12 cases
Notice is the whole game
A seat change told fourteen days out produces almost no complaints. The same change discovered at the door produces one complaint per affected guest and a refund for a third of them.
Every item has an owner

Matching rules, in order
1Keep the party together, whatever else changes.
2Preserve access facts: steps, level entry, companion adjacency.
3Equal or better band, never worse, never a surcharge.

Moving guests to the same seat number on the other side of the aisle produced almost no replies. Use mirroring as the first matching rule in every future run.

change

Gift bookings notified the wrong person
Three of the eleven who arrived unaware were attending on a ticket bought by somebody else. Notices should go to the buyer and the holder.
change
4

Six bounced emails were covered by text within minutes. Collect a mobile number as standard for every booking, optional but asked for.
change

A maintenance ticket existed and nobody connected it to ticketed seats. Maintenance tickets on seating areas should raise a disruption automatically.
