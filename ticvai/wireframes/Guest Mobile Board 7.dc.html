The platform does the chasing
The unpleasant part of organising six people is asking four of them for money twice. The reminder is sent by the venue on a fixed schedule, so the booker's relationship with their friends is not the collection mechanism. If the split fails the booking still stands and the booker pays the balance — the seats are never released because a friend was slow.

Two reminders is the cap. A third does not raise collection and does damage the venue's standing with somebody who is not even its customer.
Paying and ticketing are one link
The recipient's link carries both the ticket and the amount owed, so there is no separate payment request to lose. Their ticket works whether or not they have paid — the money is between them and the booker, and the platform will not hold a seat hostage at a door over £32.55.

The search is for the group, not for the space
Most systems treat a wheelchair space as its own inventory: the guest books W1, then hunts for five seats and finds them in another block. Here the unit of search is the party — a space with five adjacent seats, or nothing. The third option is shown greyed out and named as not offered, because a group being split is a decision the venue has made rather than a fact of the building.

Expiring vouchers are revenue the venue books by disappointing somebody at a counter. The platform does not implement expiry at all, so a tenant cannot switch it on. What is left over stays as account credit with its origin named, exactly like a cancellation refund.
