Status

Adam starts with every address allowed and every sign-in logged. It stays that way until you switch it on here — the point of the log below is that by then you can choose the networks from where people have actually been, rather than from memory.

Adam thinks you are on the same machine as the server. That is right on a workstation. On a server it means the proxy in front of Adam is not passing X-Real-IP, so everybody resolves to this one address — the log below would be a single row and these rules would mean nothing. Fix that before switching anything on.

ADAM_IP_ALLOWLIST is set to off on the server. The policy below is being ignored for every request, whatever it says. Unset it and restart to put the rules back in force.

Allowed networks

One address, written 203.0.113.7, or a range, written 203.0.113.0/24. A label is for you — a rule nobody can attribute is a rule nobody dares delete.

Who would be locked out

Worked out from where people have actually signed in, not from a list of rules. Somebody with one covered address is fine however many other addresses they also use; somebody with none has nowhere to sign in from. Accounts nobody has seen anywhere are listed separately, because they read as fine and are not.

Where people sign in from

One row per account and address: since when, how often, and most recently when. Rolled up rather than one row per request — the question is where somebody works, not what they clicked.

Turned away

Requests no rule covered. While the allowlist is off these are would have been — the dry run, written by real traffic. Repeats from one address inside a minute count once, so a scanner cannot bury the rest.
