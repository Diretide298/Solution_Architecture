Reading the accounts…

    Accounts

    An account is disabled rather than deleted, because a verdict points at the person who gave it. End sessions signs somebody out of every browser and stops there: the answer to a lost laptop, not to somebody leaving. Disable also stops them signing back in. Projects are the ADAM projects they can open; an administrator opens every one.

    Invite someone

    The address is fixed here, by you, and cannot be changed by whoever opens the link. That is what makes an invite count as having verified it, so send the link however you already talk to them. It works once, and only for that address. They see the project you choose and no other; give them more from Accounts once they are in.

    A client is outside the company. They read everything except Decisions (the ADRs, the registers and the conflict log). They can record a verdict, kept as the client review rather than the team's, and they cannot close an item. The @softlabsgroup.com rule does not apply, so the address can be anywhere; you typing it is what vouches for it. The link expires in three days whatever is chosen above.

    Not stored and not recoverable: only a hash of it is kept, so that a copy of the database cannot be used to make accounts. If it is lost, withdraw it below and make another.

    Projects

    Which OpenProject project each ADAM project reads its work from. A developer's board, a work package lookup and a new link only see work in the project chosen here, so TICVAI tickets never mix with another product's. A project with nothing chosen shows no work at all. The list of choices comes from OpenProject with your own token; no token stored yet? Add one on your settings page.

    Platforms

    Which slice of the delivery each team lead and developer owns: a side of the house, and a platform within it, or all platforms on that side, which is the grant that covers one added next month without anybody coming back here.

    This is not about what they can see. A team lead reads every change request and all of the activity on the project either way. What a platform decides is narrower: which requests reach their bell, which they may take on, and which they may settle.

    Only the System Architect hands these out. You can see who holds what.

    Download a date range

    A month of a register, as a CSV. The reviews page exports the rows it is showing you; this one takes a span of dates whether or not anybody has it on screen, which is what a report at the end of a month is made of. Same columns, same wording, so the two files stack.

    Both dates are included, whole. A row recorded at any time on the To day is in the file. Leave From empty for everything from the beginning, or To empty for everything up to now; the filename says which range you took either way. Dates are read in UTC, which is the only clock anything here is stamped with.

    Upload a decision sheet

    The other end of the download. Take a range, fill in the decision column against the rows that have been dealt with, and upload it here: the CSV as it came, or the same sheet saved as .xlsx. Nothing is written until the second press. Checking the file reports what it would do and changes nothing; a blank cell is no decision, so a sheet you have not touched does nothing at all. Rows are matched by the id column.

    Which register is the sheet from?

    Fill in our verdict against the items that have been dealt with: Built, Wired, Answered, Accepted or Approved, no action. Items already closed are left alone: closing them again would replace who closed them and when with you and today. A file exported before the id column existed cannot be applied; take the range again.

    Download the range with Change requests chosen, fill in our decision against the ones that have been settled (Accepted, Rejected or Done), the reason in because, and the commit, ADR or contract version in reference. A rejection needs a reason, here or already stored, because that is what the person who raised it reads.

    A file can answer an open request and complete an accepted one. It cannot reopen a settled request or overrule how one was already answered; those are listed for you to look at and left untouched, because they are decisions about a single request and are made on it, on the changes page.
